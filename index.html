A tribute · 15 November 1891 – 14 October 1944

Erwin Rommel
the Desert Fox

Rommel is the commander I admire most in military history. He led from the front, wrote a book about what he learned as a young officer and became famous in the desert of North Africa. I made this page to tell his story with real photos and maps, and with a source next to every date.

Start with his life ↓

01 / His life

From Heidenheim to field marshal.

Every row has its source in brackets. DHM is the Deutsches Historisches Museum (LeMO biography) and USHMM is the United States Holocaust Memorial Museum. Both are listed at the end of the page.

  1. 15 Nov 1891

    Born in Heidenheim

    Erwin Rommel was born in Heidenheim, in the kingdom of Württemberg. [DHM]

  2. 1910–1912

    Joins the army

    He joined Württemberg's Infantry Regiment No. 124 as an officer cadet (Fahnenjunker) in 1910 and became a lieutenant in 1912. [DHM]

  3. Nov 1916

    Marries Lucie

    He married Lucie Maria Mollin in Danzig. They had one son, Manfred, who was later mayor of Stuttgart for many years. [DHM; Wikipedia]

  4. 1917

    Italy and the Pour le Mérite

    In October 1917 his unit was sent to the Isonzo front in Italy. For his actions in the Caporetto offensive, including Monte Matajur and Longarone, he received the Pour le Mérite in December 1917, Prussia's highest award for bravery at the time. [DHM; USHMM; Wikipedia]

  5. 1937

    Infanterie greift an

    He published Infanterie greift an (Infantry Attacks), a book about his combat experience in the First World War. [DHM; Wikipedia]

  6. 1938–1939

    Commander of Hitler's escort

    From October 1938 he commanded the unit that escorted Hitler, in the Sudetenland, in Prague and during the invasion of Poland. [DHM; USHMM]

  7. May 1940

    7th Panzer Division

    He led the 7th Panzer Division in the campaign in France. The division moved so fast that it was called the "Ghost Division". [DHM; USHMM; Wikipedia]

  8. Feb 1941

    Africa

    He took command of the German Afrikakorps in Libya. The North Africa section below tells this part. [DHM; USHMM]

  9. 22 Jun 1942

    Field marshal

    After the capture of Tobruk, Hitler promoted him to Generalfeldmarschall. [DHM]

  10. Nov 1943

    The Atlantic coast

    He was ordered to inspect the defences on the French Atlantic coast. [DHM]

  11. Jul 1944

    Wounded in Normandy

    Low-flying Allied aircraft attacked his car near Caen and he was badly wounded. [DHM; USHMM]

  12. 14 Oct 1944

    Death in Herrlingen

    Two generals sent by the high command came to his home in Herrlingen. He was forced to take poison. A state ceremony followed in Ulm on 18 October. [DHM; USHMM]

02 / North Africa, 1941–1943

The Afrikakorps and the desert war.

In February 1941 Rommel took command of the German forces sent to Libya to help their Italian allies. [DHM; USHMM] In the desert he became known as the "Desert Fox", a name his enemies used too. [USHMM]

What I find most interesting is how he commanded. He spent a lot of time near the front in his command vehicle, like in the photo, instead of staying at a headquarters far behind. That let him decide fast, but it also made him dependent on supplies that came across the Mediterranean and were never enough.

His biggest success was the capture of Tobruk in June 1942, and on 22 June Hitler made him a field marshal. [DHM] In October 1942 the British attacked at El Alamein, in Egypt. In November, after the British broke through, Rommel began the retreat. [DHM] In March 1943 he left North Africa. [DHM]

03 / The Atlantic Wall, 1943–1944

Waiting for the invasion.

The Atlantic Wall was the line of bunkers, guns and beach obstacles that Germany built along the coast of occupied Western Europe. In November 1943 Rommel was ordered to inspect these defences on the French Atlantic coast. [DHM]

The photos from those months show him on beaches and dunes, walking the coast with his officers. In the one below you can see the wooden posts that were put on the beaches to stop landing craft.

The Allies landed in Normandy on 6 June 1944. In July, while the fighting was still going on, Rommel was badly wounded when aircraft attacked his car near Caen. [DHM; Wikipedia]

04 / 1944 and the debate

How it ended, and what is still discussed.

Rommel served Hitler's regime and was close to Hitler for years. After the failed attempt on Hitler's life on 20 July 1944, the regime accused him of being involved, and on 14 October 1944 he was forced to take poison; the regime then hid the truth behind a state funeral. Historians still debate how close he was to Nazism and how much he took part in the plot. [USHMM; DHM]
